# What is the Blipdeck
Do you like collecting physical retro games but still prefer emulating them on your PC? Then you should build yourself a Blipdeck or similar. This is a personal project and not something for sale, but I will be sharing the journey here. 

Blipdeck is a small physical box that contains:

* A card reader
* A USB Hub
* A DVD Reader
* An NFC Reader

Using either custom Blipdeck software installed on your computer or something like [Zaparoo](https://zaparoo.org/downloads/), it is then possible to launch a game by simply using an NFC tag or disc after you have mapped the games. NFC stickers will be used to attach them to the cartridges.

What Blipdeck provides is the physical interface for this to work and to give me the feeling of "using" my actual cartridges and disks, instead of simply booting the emulator, without actually playing from them. Personally, I love using [RetroAchievements](https://retroachievements.org/) and dumping the actual roms from the cartridge will not always work for that, hence why mapping the cartridge to a rom file on my computer works best for my use case.

# Physical Game Releases

The BlipDeck project is not just about existing games. It's also something that will get "physical" releases, simply by putting the game on sd-cards and giving them a nice packaging with inside a switch-sized case. Templates for covers and manuals will be developed and uploaded for anyone to use, and also the finished covers and manuals for all "releases".

# Status & Roadmap

3D designs are done, so what is next on the roadmap is:

* 3D printing the blipdeck case and putting the parts together
* Develop software for the Blipping mechanism.
* Creating the manual for BliPdeck and printing it (will also be uploaded)
* Determining and announcing the 12-15 Launch Games for the system.
* Prepare the discs and cartridges I own and map using the software.
* Launch before Christmas 2027

# Why do all this?
This is not for purists and those who want the best possible retro experience. If so, playing a game on original hardware is unbeatable and I totally get it. This is also not for the person who enjoys emulating and is looking for more convenience around it. Actually, this project is in a way making it less convenient to play. This is for me. And for anyone like me. Anyone that is looking for that old school feeling of popping a game cartridge in and (not really but close enough) using your physical media, but still keeping all the benefits of emulation. And together with that... also expand my physical library with (fake) game releases. It's childish I know, but hey, so is playing video games.
